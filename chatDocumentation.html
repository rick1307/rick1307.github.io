Community Architecture

The Wild Ledger Community Room

A study document capturing the ideas behind community chat, Member Record access, temporary identity, Community Managers, XRPL recognition, and XRP tipping.

The idea in one sentence: Wild Ledger owns the machinery, each community owns its experience, the Member Record controls the door, and a temporary nickname controls what other people see in the room.

1. What are we actually building?

Are we building a Leaper Lounge chat application?

Not exactly. Leaper Lounge is the first community experience built on a shared Wild Ledger community-room engine. The underlying system should not be hard-coded for Sailing Frog's Leap.

So is there one Wild Ledger chat room?

There is one shared chat system, but users experience separate community rooms. Every message, presence record, event, and permission belongs to a community ID. The system only delivers the records belonging to the community the member has entered.

SFL → LEAPER-LOUNGE → "Great sunset tonight!" ZHR → GEAR-NUT-GARAGE → "What engine is in that car?"

Those conversations can happen at the same time on the same Wild Ledger infrastructure without either community seeing the other.

Why build it that way?

Because the second, third, or hundredth Wild Ledger community should not require another chat application. A new community gets its own identity, room name, manager, rules, assets, events, and branding while using the same underlying engine.

2. Who owns what?

Wild Ledger owns the machinery

Community isolation, realtime messaging, presence, manager authorization, Member Record gating, XRPL account association, awards, tipping infrastructure, events, moderation, and other shared capabilities.

The community owns the experience

The room name, branding, Community Manager identity, community asset, badges, events, language, livestream experience, and personality.

Wild Ledger
↓
Community — e.g. Sailing Frog's Leap
↓
Member Record
↓
Community Room — e.g. Leaper Lounge
Important: A Leaper should feel like they are still inside Sailing Frog's Leap. Wild Ledger can power the experience without forcing the user to leave the community experience.

3. What controls the door?

Can anybody simply browse into Leaper Lounge?

No. In the intended model, the Member Record is the gate. Wild Ledger must establish that the XRPL account belongs to a legitimate member of that specific community before granting access to its room.

If someone is a Sailing Frog's Leap member, can they enter Zach's room?

Not because of their SFL membership. Membership is community-specific. An account that legitimately belongs to both communities can enter both, but each community relationship stands on its own.

XRPL account + valid SFL Member Record → Leaper Lounge access XRPL account + valid ZHR Member Record → Gear Nut Garage access

4. What identity do people see?

Does the room show a member's XRPL address?

No. The XRPL account can be the persistent machinery underneath the experience without becoming the person's public social identity.

What does everyone else see?

A temporary nickname chosen for that visit. It does not need to persist. Leave the room and the nickname can disappear.

Could the same person use different names in different communities?

Yes. Someone could be SaltyDog in Leaper Lounge and BigBlockBob in Gear Nut Garage. Wild Ledger may know which authorized account is behind each session when necessary, but the communities should not be given a public cross-community identity link.

Persistent ledger identity underneath. Temporary social identity on top.

5. What is a Community Manager?

Who is the Community Manager?

The Community Manager is an authorized role in Wild Ledger. For Sailing Frog's Leap, that role is held by the authorized Sailing Frog's Leap manager account. Another community has its own manager or managers.

Why is that important?

Because Wild Ledger should never have special code that means “Rick can do this.” It should understand that an authenticated Community Manager can perform certain actions inside the community they manage.

Can there eventually be more than one manager?

The architecture should allow it. A community might eventually authorize another person to moderate, manage an event, or perform selected community functions without sharing another manager's wallet credentials.

Does typing the manager's nickname give someone manager powers?

No. A manager name can be reserved for appearance, but privileges must come from secure manager authorization. A nickname is never authority.

6. What does manager presence mean?

Can members tell when the Community Manager is in the room?

Yes. Presence is a shared Wild Ledger capability that each community can express in its own voice. Sailing Frog's Leap might say “Sailing Frog's Leap is aboard.” Zach's Hot Rods might say “Zach is in the Garage.”

Is manager presence the same as being live on video?

No. Presence and broadcasting are separate states. The manager can enter the room, type, moderate, or award recognition without automatically starting a broadcast.

7. Where does YouTube fit?

Does Wild Ledger need to build its own video streaming system?

Not necessarily. The current concept is that YouTube can provide the broadcast infrastructure while the community's Wild Ledger room provides the participation experience.

YouTube

Video/audio broadcast, bandwidth, distribution, and livestream delivery.

Wild Ledger

Member-gated chat, temporary nicknames, manager presence, recognition, XRP tipping, events, and community history.

Working concept: YouTube is the broadcast infrastructure. The community room is where the community participates.

8. How does value move?

What moves from the Community Manager to a member?

Recognition moves outward. In Sailing Frog's Leap, the manager can recognize participation by issuing LEAP using an XRPL Check. A Great Chat recognition uses the agreed protocol meaning:

SFL-LEAP/BADGE = GREAT-CHAT

The member's XRPL destination is already associated with the authorized Member Record, so the manager should not have to copy and paste an address while participating in the room.

What moves from a member to the Community Manager?

Appreciation moves inward. A member can voluntarily tip the Community Manager in XRP. The member approves the XRPL transaction from their own wallet; Wild Ledger should not take custody of the member's funds or private keys.

RECOGNITION

Manager → Member

LEAP via XRPL Check

Example meaning: GREAT-CHAT

APPRECIATION

Member → Manager

XRP tip

Voluntary support sent to the Community Manager.

Recognition flows outward in LEAP. Appreciation can flow inward in XRP.

9. What happens when the manager sees a great comment?

What should the interaction feel like?

Fast. The manager taps the member and chooses a standardized recognition such as Great Chat. Wild Ledger already knows the eligible recipient account and prepares the correct award transaction and protocol data. The manager approves it in the wallet and returns to the conversation.

Can the recognition become part of the member's history?

Yes. Once the relevant XRPL transaction is validated and recognized by the Wild Ledger/SFL protocol, that participation can be reflected in the member's Crew Record rather than disappearing as a disposable chat moment.

10. How separate are communities really?

Is filtering messages in JavaScript enough?

No. Community isolation belongs in the backend authorization and data rules. An SFL browser should not receive ZHR messages and merely hide them. It should only be permitted to receive the community data it is authorized to access.

What should remain separate?

Messages, temporary nicknames, presence, manager status, live status, events, community-specific awards, moderation controls, and other community activity should all be scoped to the appropriate community.

11. What should be shared across Wild Ledger?

If communities are isolated, what is the advantage of one platform?

The engine is reusable. Wild Ledger can provide the same underlying capabilities to every participating community while letting each one feel completely different to its members.

Wild Ledger ├── Community │ ├── Manager(s) │ ├── Member Records │ ├── Community Room │ ├── Events │ ├── Challenges │ ├── Badges / Recognition │ └── Community configuration └── Shared XRPL-aware infrastructure

12. Why “Powered by the XRP Ledger”?

Is that just branding?

The intent is for it to describe the underlying architecture. Wild Ledger can use the XRP Ledger for the durable value and participation layer while keeping the visible community experience approachable and community-specific.

Does every interaction have to be on-ledger?

No. Realtime chat, temporary nicknames, presence, interface state, and similar social functions belong off-ledger. XRPL is used where a durable ledger action has meaning—for example XRP transfers and protocol-defined community recognition.

13. The mental model to remember

Wild Ledger owns the machinery.
The community owns the experience.
The Member Record controls the door.
The temporary nickname controls what people see.
The Community Manager is a role, not a hard-coded person.
Communities share infrastructure but not their conversations.
Recognition can flow Manager → Member in the community asset.
Appreciation can flow Member → Manager in XRP.
The XRP Ledger carries durable value and recognition; the room carries the conversation.

14. What this means for the first build

Do we throw away the Leaper Lounge prototype?

No. Its visible experience remains the foundation. What changes is our understanding of the machinery behind it: the production version should be the first community implementation of a reusable Wild Ledger room engine.

What is the architectural target?

Build the shared Wild Ledger community-room backend from the beginning, with Sailing Frog's Leap as the first configured community and Leaper Lounge as its first room. Keep community isolation, Member Record gating, manager authorization, and ledger/social identity separation foundational rather than adding them later.
