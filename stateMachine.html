Given that shared digital state exists...

The LEAP Logbook

The infrastructure already exists. It can hold shared, permanent, machine-readable state that independent software can read later. So the next question is smaller and much more practical: what can Sailing Frog's Leap do with a tiny slice of that capacity?

Our answer is simple: use that capacity as a crew logbook. When a Leaper does something worth remembering, the recognition should not disappear with the episode. It should become part of the record.
01

Start with the use, not the infrastructure.

We already have the infrastructure. Now give it a job.

For Sailing Frog's Leap, that job is participation. People watch the adventure. Some of them do more than watch: they answer a challenge, help solve a problem, show up for an event, contribute an idea, join a poll, take part in the story, or earn a piece of recognition tied to the adventure.

That is the community we want to build around. We only need a narrow slice of shared ledger capacity to remember what happened. The blockchain is simply the logbook underneath it.

WatchFollow the Sailing Frog's Leap adventure.
EngageTake part in a challenge, event, poll, idea, or community moment.
EarnReceive LEAP, rank, or recognition for showing up.
Build historyParticipation becomes cumulative instead of disposable.
02

Most online participation disappears.

A comment is pushed down the page. A poll closes. A live chat ends. A great contribution becomes another moment buried in another feed.

That makes participation feel temporary even when the relationship is not.

Badges make participation cumulative rather than disposable.

LEAP applies the same idea more broadly. Membership, rank, badges, challenges, events, and origin stories can accumulate into a personal crew history that stays with the member's public XRPL account.

03

Use a little bandwidth to create a lot of memory.

Every tap into shared state has limits. For LEAP, we do not need much.

We use the XRP Ledger and a small amount of transaction memo bandwidth. When Sailing Frog's Leap creates a LEAP-related ledger event, the transaction can carry a short, standardized memo. The validated ledger supplies the permanent transaction record and timestamp. The memo tells software what the event means.

ACCOUNTMember's public XRPL account

The amount can be small. The important thing is the durable, machine-readable record that says: this happened.

04

That is a state transition.

Before the ledger event, that recognition is not part of the member's public LEAP history. After validation, it is.

State AThe member is part of the crew, but the recognition is not yet recorded.
→
TransitionSailing Frog's Leap writes a standardized LEAP event to the XRPL.
→
State BThe public ledger now contains permanent evidence of that recognition.

We are using globally shared infrastructure for a very parochial purpose: keeping the crew logbook for one sailing community.

05

The logbook can describe different kinds of crew state.

The useful part is not one special memo. It is a consistent language that lets the website, Discord, or future software understand what kind of event it is reading.

SFL-LEAP/BADGEPermanent recognition for something a member did.
SFL-LEAP/EVENTA shared moment tied to the Sailing Frog's Leap adventure.
SFL-LEAP/CHALLENGEAn episode or community challenge and its numbered history.
SFL-LEAP/ORIGINFounding or origin recognition such as LEAP-OG.
SFL-LEAP/PROTOCOLThe version of the LEAP convention used to interpret the record.

The namespace makes the history sortable and searchable by machines. The ledger timestamp already tells us when the event was validated, so the event name does not need to carry redundant dates.

06

Current status and permanent history are different.

Some parts of LEAP describe where a member stands right now. Other parts record what that member has done and been part of.

Current status

Membership / Access Holding LEAP tells the system that this public account is part of the crew and can be used to determine what member experiences are available now.
Rank Current LEAP holdings determine the member's present rank: Deckhand, Bosun, Quartermaster, First Mate, or Master of the Leap.

Permanent history

Badges Permanent recognition for what the member actually did.
Events Shared moments that become part of the member's personal history with the adventure.
Challenges completed A lasting record of the challenges the member took part in and completed.

Membership / Access and Rank describe where you are now. Badges, Events, and Challenges Completed describe what you have done and been part of.

Current status can change. History does not. That is what makes the ledger useful as a logbook.

07

Machine-readable history changes the engagement loop.

A normal audience interaction ends when the interaction ends. A recorded LEAP interaction can become input for the next experience.

A Leaper participates
↓ The participation is recognized
↓ The recognition enters the public logbook
↓ Software can read that history later
↓ The next experience can build on everything that came before

A future page might display a badge automatically. A Discord app might recognize a rank. A new challenge might be available only to members with a particular history. The important thing is that the system does not have to forget between episodes.

08

This is tiny. That is the point.

Shared digital infrastructure becomes useful one local use at a time.

For Sailing Frog's Leap, the use case is deliberately small: a membership credential, a rank, a badge, a challenge, an event, a line in the crew logbook.

The pattern is simple: a shared current state, a trusted transition, and a durable new state that independent software can read later.

The infrastructureBlockspace records shared, durable state across independent actors.
Local useLEAP uses that capacity to remember who came aboard, what they did, and how their history with the adventure grew.
The LEAP experiment

Turn the adventure into a history people can build.

Watching the adventure is one thing. Becoming part of it is another.

LEAP uses a small piece of shared blockspace to turn community participation into a permanent crew logbook—one that people can see, software can read, and future experiences can build upon.

The blockchain is not the experience. It is the memory underneath the experience.
