LEAP Crew Ranks

Rank tells your role

LEAP ranks are a simple way to recognize how deeply someone has become part of the Sailing Frog's Leap crew. Your current LEAP balance determines your rank. What you actually did along the way is recorded separately through badges and recognition.

Rank is not a score for who is “better.” It is a visible measure of sustained participation in the LEAP crew system.

The first LEAP changes the relationship. You are no longer simply watching from shore. You are aboard.

From there, rank gives the community a shared language for recognizing the people who keep showing up, contributing, participating, and helping shape what Sailing Frog's Leap becomes.

Rank tells how much LEAP you hold. Badges tell what happened.

The crew ladder

The system deliberately starts simple. Five permanent rank levels create progression without turning LEAP into a complicated game.

1–4 LEAP

Deckhand

“I’m officially part of the crew.”

⚓

What it means

Deckhand is the moment a viewer crosses the line from audience to crew. One LEAP is enough. It says: I showed up, I joined, and I now have a permanent place in the LEAP community.

How someone gets here

Receive a first LEAP through the official distribution process, earn one through participation, or otherwise become an approved holder in the crew system.

What it may unlock

  • Official recognition as a crew member
  • Entry into crew-only community spaces
  • Eligibility for participation badges
  • Access to basic member polls or crew questions
  • A permanent starting point in the LEAP logbook
5–9 LEAP

Bosun

“I’m not just aboard. I keep showing up.”

🪢

What it means

A Bosun is becoming a familiar hand. This is the first rank that suggests repeated participation rather than a single moment of joining. The person is beginning to become part of the fabric of the crew.

How someone gets here

Continued involvement: useful comments, livestream participation, answering questions, helping other members, contributing ideas, or simply being a dependable presence over time.

What it may unlock

  • Visible Bosun role in community spaces
  • Occasional public recognition
  • Expanded access to crew discussions
  • Participation in selected polls or planning questions
  • Early eligibility for special crew activities
10–24 LEAP

Quartermaster

“I have history here, and my voice matters.”

🧭

What it means

Quartermaster represents an established supporter. At this point, the member has accumulated enough LEAP that their connection to the channel is clearly more than casual. They have a recognizable history within the community.

How someone gets here

By steadily contributing value to the community and building a meaningful LEAP balance over time. There is no requirement to be loud or constantly online—useful participation matters more than noise.

What it may unlock

  • Quartermaster status in Discord or other community spaces
  • Access to member polls and channel questions
  • Priority consideration for Q&A topics
  • Participation in selected planning discussions
  • Recognition as an established member of the crew
25–50 LEAP

First Mate

“I’m part of the inner crew.”

⛵

What it means

First Mate is an inner-circle rank. A holder at this level has demonstrated sustained commitment to the LEAP community and has likely accumulated a meaningful history of participation, recognition, or both.

How someone gets here

Through long-term participation and repeated contributions that matter to the channel or the crew. First Mate should feel earned rather than merely collected.

What it may unlock

  • Priority Q&A or question submission
  • Occasional early access to content or announcements
  • First Mate community spaces
  • Greater influence in selected crew polls
  • Public recognition as an inner-circle supporter
51+ LEAP

Master of the Leap

“I’ve become part of the history of this community.”

🐸

What it means

Master of the Leap is the highest permanent crew rank. It is intentionally open-ended: once someone reaches 51 LEAP, the emphasis should shift away from chasing another title and toward the history they continue to build.

How someone gets here

By becoming one of the most deeply involved members of the LEAP community over time. The title recognizes sustained participation, accumulated LEAP, and a durable connection to Sailing Frog's Leap.

What it may unlock

  • Highest permanent community rank
  • Special public recognition
  • Access to selected inner-circle discussions or events
  • Priority participation in experiments and new LEAP features
  • A visible place among the long-term core crew

Three rules keep the rank system honest

The rank ladder works best when everybody understands exactly what it is—and what it is not.

Rule one

Rank follows current holdings

If someone holds 12 LEAP, they are a Quartermaster. If their balance later falls to 7, their current rank becomes Bosun. The ledger provides the current state.

Rule two

Badges preserve history

Rank can change. Historical recognition does not need to disappear. A voyage, milestone, or contribution can remain part of the member's logbook even if their current rank changes.

Rule three

Participation beats noise

LEAP should reward useful involvement, not simply whoever posts most often. Good questions, help, ideas, information, and meaningful participation should matter.

Ranks and badges are deliberately different.

Rank answers: “How much LEAP does this person currently hold?”

A badge answers: “What happened? What did this person do, experience, help with, or become part of?”

A Deckhand could therefore have a rare Panama Canal badge. A Master of the Leap might have dozens of badges. One system shows current status; the other preserves history.

“Rank tells where you stand today. The logbook tells the story of how you got here.”
— Sailing Frog's Leap

Common questions

The pilot can evolve, but these are the basic principles.

Do I need to buy LEAP?The concept is built around participation and recognition. LEAP can be distributed or earned according to the rules Sailing Frog's Leap develops for the community.
Can my rank go down?Yes. Rank reflects your current LEAP balance. If LEAP leaves the wallet being used for membership, the rank associated with that wallet can change.
Do I lose my badges if rank changes?The intent is no. Badges and memo-based recognition are historical records, while rank is current state.
Does a higher rank make someone “more important”?No. It reflects deeper participation in this particular membership system. Good communities still need new Deckhands, quiet contributors, occasional viewers, and people with very different levels of involvement.
Will every privilege be automatic?Not initially. During the pilot, some recognition may be manual. If the system proves useful, wallet verification and automatic community roles can be added later.
