Wild Ledger · LEAP Frequently Asked Questions

LEAP FAQ

These are the rules and mechanics that apply across participating LEAP communities. A community can create its own experience, but the LEAP system underneath works the same way.

Getting started

The basics

What is LEAP?LEAP is a membership and recognition token used across participating Wild Ledger communities. Each community decides what participation means inside its own community, while LEAP provides the common system underneath.
Do I have to buy LEAP?No. LEAP is a membership and recognition token. When you earn LEAP, a participating community can offer it to you by LEAP Check.
Can I transfer LEAP to someone else?Yes. Checks are how participating communities can distribute LEAP from a distributor. Once you cash a Check, that LEAP is in your wallet and the Check has done its job. If transfers are enabled, you can send LEAP directly to another LEAP holder — keep it, share it with another member, or give some to someone new.
Is XRP the same as LEAP?No. XRP is the native asset of the XRP Ledger. LEAP is the membership and recognition token that lives on the XRP Ledger.
Is LEAP the same across participating communities?Yes. LEAP is the common system. The communities are different. Each participating community can create its own activities, recognition, badges, challenges, events, ranks, or other ways to take part.
Wallet, privacy & cost

Why LEAP uses an XRPL address

Your public address is not your wallet secret.

LEAP only needs the public XRP Ledger address you choose to use for your Member Record. Wild Ledger and participating communities never need your recovery words, private key, family seed, or recovery phrase.

For privacy, a separate XRPL wallet for LEAP is recommended instead of your primary crypto wallet. Xaman also makes managing multiple XRP wallets easy.

Why does LEAP need my XRP Ledger address?Your public XRPL address is how LEAP knows which Member Record is yours. Think of it as the number on your membership passport. LEAP uses that address to find your LEAP balance, rank, badges, and history on the public XRP Ledger. You do not create a separate LEAP username or password.
Is my XRPL address private?No. An XRPL address and the transactions associated with it are public on the XRP Ledger. That is an important thing to understand before you join.
If I give LEAP my XRPL address, can someone see what that wallet has done?Yes. The XRP Ledger is public, so anyone who knows an XRPL address can view the public activity associated with that address. That is also why a separate wallet for LEAP is recommended. LEAP does not need to know anything about your other crypto activity.
Is Wild Ledger building a database that connects my identity to my XRPL address?No. LEAP's external persistence layer is the XRP Ledger blockchain. A community Join page can ask for the public XRPL address you choose to use with LEAP so your first LEAP Check can be processed. Your Member Record itself is built from public XRP Ledger data, not from a private LEAP database of names and balances.
What personal information does LEAP actually need from me?For the LEAP system itself, the website needs the public XRPL address you choose to use with LEAP. LEAP does not need your private key, recovery phrase, banking information, government ID, or the address of your primary crypto wallet.
Why do you recommend a separate wallet for LEAP?A public blockchain address can reveal its public transaction history, and LEAP does not need to know anything about your other crypto activity. A dedicated LEAP wallet creates a clean boundary: your LEAP membership activity stays in one wallet, and everything else stays somewhere else.
Can Wild Ledger or a participating community access or control my wallet?No. Knowing your public XRPL address does not give anyone control of your wallet. You control it with your private keys. Never send Wild Ledger, a participating community, or anyone else your recovery words, private key, family seed, or recovery phrase.
Do I have to put a lot of XRP in this wallet?No. XRP pays the XRP Ledger's very small transaction fees and supports its reserve requirements. The current LEAP guidance is to keep enough XRP in the dedicated wallet to satisfy the XRP Ledger reserve and the small transaction costs needed to use LEAP. Reserve requirements can change, and XRP held for reserve is not a LEAP membership fee.
Why do you recommend Xaman?Xaman is currently the wallet Wild Ledger supports because it gives members a clear path through the XRP Ledger features LEAP uses, including enabling LEAP and cashing LEAP Checks. Xaman also makes managing multiple wallets easy, which supports a simple habit: keep long-term savings separate from the wallet you use for transactions. Supporting one wallet first also lets participating communities give everyone the same simple instructions.
Do I have to use Xaman?LEAP is built on the XRP Ledger, not on Xaman. Your Member Record is tied to your XRPL address. Xaman is simply the wallet currently supported for the full LEAP process. Other XRPL wallets can be supported as their handling of the LEAP steps is verified.
Why not just use an email address and password?Because LEAP is designed so the XRP Ledger itself is the permanent logbook. Your public XRPL address connects you to your Member Record without requiring a separate LEAP username and password system. The community website is the front door, your LEAP record is the passport, and the XRP Ledger is the logbook.
LEAP Checks

About the Checks

What is a LEAP Check?A LEAP Check uses the XRP Ledger's built-in Check feature. It lets a participating community offer you LEAP without simply pushing tokens into your wallet. You choose whether to cash the Check and accept the LEAP.
Why use Checks instead of just sending LEAP?Checks let you choose whether to accept each LEAP award. They also give each award its own record on the XRP Ledger. Once you cash the Check, the LEAP is in your wallet and can move like any other LEAP.
Do I need to set up LEAP before I receive a Check?No separate LEAP setup is required before a community sends the Check. Xaman guides you through what is needed when you cash it.
Do I have to cash a Check?No. It is your wallet. A pending Check is an offer of LEAP. You choose whether to cash it and accept the LEAP. A LEAP Check can also carry the reason it was earned along with the LEAP itself, preserving that recognition as part of the XRP Ledger record.
How do I see LEAP Checks waiting for me?Your community's Member Record can list LEAP Checks that are currently waiting for you to cash. A Check remains outstanding until it is cashed or canceled.
How do I cash a Check?LEAP currently uses Xaman for Check cashing. Your community can guide you to the Check in Xaman so you can review it and choose whether to accept it.
Member Record

Your LEAP record

What does my Member Record show?Your Member Record can show your current LEAP balance and rank, outstanding LEAP Checks waiting for you, and the LEAP history you have actually earned on the XRP Ledger.
What is LEAP Waiting for You?It is the list of LEAP Checks that are currently available for you to cash. A Check stays there while it is outstanding and disappears after it is cashed or canceled.
When does something become part of my LEAP history?A Check-based recognition becomes part of your earned record after that Check is successfully cashed. A Check that is still waiting or that was canceled does not count as earned.
What happens to old canceled Checks?They remain part of the public XRP Ledger transaction history, but they are not shown as earned badges or recognitions in your personal Member Record.
Technical details

Under the hood

You do not need to understand any of this to use LEAP. This section is for anyone interested in how LEAP uses the XRP Ledger under the hood.

Memos & Transactions

Where does the memo actually live?The memo belongs to the CheckCreate transaction that creates the Check. It does not belong to an individual LEAP token or to the Check object itself. Cashing uses a separate CheckCash transaction, and canceling uses a separate CheckCancel transaction. The original CheckCreate and its memo remain in public ledger history either way.
Does the memo travel with one specific LEAP coin?No. LEAP is fungible. The memo belongs to the transaction record, not to one individually serialized LEAP unit.
Why use community namespaces?A namespace lets LEAP software separate a community's records from unrelated wallet activity and organize them into the community's badges, events, challenges, member history, and administrative history. Each community can use its own namespace and conventions.
