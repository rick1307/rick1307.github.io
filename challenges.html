LEAP Challenges

Challenges

Challenges turn watching into doing. They give members a clear objective and a temporary opportunity to participate, accomplish something, and add that accomplishment to their community history.

What a challenge is

A challenge is a temporary invitation to go do something specific. It opens, stays active for a while, and eventually closes — but the end does not always have to be known when it begins.

Idea one

Do something real

A challenge should ask for action — not just another click. It might involve trying something, making something, helping, exploring, solving, or participating.

Idea two

Know what counts

Every challenge should make the objective clear, say who can take part, and explain what a member needs to do for the challenge to count. If a closing date or limit is known, that can be stated too.

Idea three

Build history

When a completed challenge is recognized, it can become part of that member's LEAP community history as a Challenge. The challenge itself may still remain open for other members until it is closed.

The end can be known — or decided later.

A challenge is temporary, but temporary does not always mean a fixed deadline. The community operator may announce the end in advance, tie it to a result or number of completions, or simply close it when the challenge has served its purpose.

How challenges work

The exact rules can change from challenge to challenge. For a member, the basic path stays simple even if the challenge remains open for other people afterward.

1

A challenge opens

The objective, what counts, and any known deadline or limit are announced.

2

You go do it

The point is participation in the real world or the real community.

3

You show what happened

If the challenge calls for proof or a story, the instructions will say what to share.

4

It joins your history

Once recognized, the Challenge can appear as part of your LEAP record.

What a challenge might look like

There are no fixed challenge types. These are simply examples of how a community operator might decide to run one.

For example

First to finish

A challenge could stay open until the first person, the first five people, or another set number of members completes it.

For example

Reach a goal

A challenge could remain active until a particular result is reached, then close once that goal has been met.

For example

Open for a while

A challenge could run until a stated date, or simply stay open until the community operator decides it has served its purpose.
