LEAP Badges & Community History

Badges

LEAP badges recognize the moments, contributions, places, milestones, and shared experiences that make one member's community history different from another's.

Three ideas behind badges

Each community operator decides what deserves recognition. These ideas are a useful starting point, not a rulebook.

Idea one

Badges recognize meaningful things

A badge can mark something worth remembering: a contribution, a milestone, a useful act, a role, a place, or a shared moment.

Idea two

Badges preserve history

Badges preserve specific recognitions in your history. Once earned, they are not erased because your wallet balance changes.

Idea three

Badges create identity

Two members at the same rank can have completely different badge histories—and therefore completely different stories.

The clearest difference is time

Badges, challenges, and events can all recognize many members. What usually separates them is how long that recognition stays open.

Open-ended

Badge

A badge has no natural end date. A community operator can keep awarding the same badge for as long as it remains meaningful.

Temporary

Challenge

A challenge is active for a while, but its end may not be known in advance. It might close after the first person, the first five people, a goal is reached, a date arrives, or simply when the operator decides it has run its course.

Fixed in time

Event

An event is tied to a particular day or defined window. You participated in that event or you did not; once it is over, that moment becomes part of the community's history.

A pattern, not a requirement

This is the general design idea, not an enforced rule. Community operators can use badges, challenges, and events in whatever way fits their community. Sometimes a recognition may later look like it would have fit better somewhere else. That is okay. The important thing is preserving what the operator intended to recognize when the record was created.

Badges you can earn

Each community can create recognitions that fit its own people, story, and experiences.

Ways badges can be used

These are simply examples of how a community might use badges. They are not fixed categories, and an operator can combine them, ignore them, or create something completely different.

⚓

Journeys & Adventures

“I was part of this chapter.”

Could recognize

Trips, routes, journeys, crossings, road trips, passages, or other shared adventures that become part of the community story.

Examples might include
  • First Road Trip
  • Coastal Passage
  • Cross-Country Run
  • Major Journey
📍

Places

“This place is part of my story.”

Could recognize

Places, venues, ports, regions, shows, meet-ups, or memorable destinations that matter to the community.

Examples might include
  • Home Port
  • First Meet-Up
  • Favorite Venue
  • Special Destination
🧭

Participation

“I showed up and took part.”

Could recognize

Showing up for livestreams, polls, special gatherings, recurring participation, challenges, or moments when members help shape the community.

Examples might include
  • Live Event
  • Community Poll
  • Launch Day
  • Watch Party
🛠️

Contributions

“I helped make something better.”

Could recognize

Useful contributions such as problem-solving, technical help, ideas, research, creative work, information, or helping another member.

Examples might include
  • Problem Solver
  • Community Contributor
  • Helping Hand
  • Useful Intel
🏁

Milestones

“I was here when it happened.”

Could recognize

Important moments in the life of the community or meaningful milestones in a member’s own history.

Examples might include
  • LEAP Genesis
  • First Community Group
  • Community Milestone
  • First LEAP Pilot
🐸

Special Recognition

“This one has a story behind it.”

Could recognize

Something unusual, personal, rare, or simply worth remembering that does not fit neatly anywhere else.

Examples might include
  • Community Recognition
  • Above & Beyond
  • LEAP OG
  • Special Project Recognition

Badges turn participation into history.

Your community history builds over time — one milestone, contribution, badge, and shared moment at a time.
