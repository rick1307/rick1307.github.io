Community Architecture

The Wild Ledger Community Room

A study document capturing the ideas behind community chat, Member Record access, temporary identity, Community Managers, XRPL recognition, and XRP tipping.

The idea in one sentence: Wild Ledger owns the machinery, each community owns its experience, the Member Record controls the door, and a temporary nickname controls what other people see in the room.

1. What are we actually building?

Are we building a Leaper Lounge chat application?

Not exactly. Leaper Lounge is the first community experience built on a shared Wild Ledger community-room engine. The underlying system should not be hard-coded for Sailing Frog's Leap.

So is there one Wild Ledger chat room?

There is one shared chat system, but users experience separate community rooms. Every message, presence record, event, and permission belongs to a community ID. The system only delivers the records belonging to the community the member has entered.

SFL → LEAPER-LOUNGE → "Great sunset tonight!" ZHR → GEAR-NUT-GARAGE → "What engine is in that car?"

Those conversations can happen at the same time on the same Wild Ledger infrastructure without either community seeing the other.

Why build it that way?

Because the second, third, or hundredth Wild Ledger community should not require another chat application. A new community gets its own identity, room name, manager, rules, assets, events, and branding while using the same underlying engine.

2. Who owns what?

Wild Ledger owns the machinery

Community isolation, realtime messaging, presence, manager authorization, Member Record gating, awards, tipping infrastructure, events, moderation, and other shared capabilities.

The community owns the experience

The room name, branding, Community Manager identity, community asset, badges, events, language, livestream experience, and personality.

Wild Ledger
↓
Community — e.g. Sailing Frog's Leap
↓
Member Record
↓
Community Room — e.g. Leaper Lounge
Important: A Leaper should feel like they are still inside Sailing Frog's Leap. Wild Ledger can power the experience without forcing the user to leave the community experience.

3. What controls the door?

Can anybody simply browse into Leaper Lounge?

No. In the intended model, the Member Record is the gate. Wild Ledger must establish that the XRPL account belongs to a legitimate member of that specific community before granting access to its room.

If someone is a Sailing Frog's Leap member, can they enter Zach's room?

Not because of their SFL membership. Membership is community-specific. An account that legitimately belongs to both communities can enter both, but each community relationship stands on its own.

XRPL account + valid SFL Member Record → Leaper Lounge access XRPL account + valid ZHR Member Record → Gear Nut Garage access

4. Participant identity and anonymity

What is the privacy objective?

Wild Ledger is designed to maximize participant anonymity. We engineer toward that objective by minimizing identity linkage.

What identity does the room use?

The Lounge uses a temporary alias chosen for that visit. The alias is the participant's social identity inside the room. It does not need to persist after the session ends.

Does the Lounge expose the participant's XRPL address?

No. An XRPL address must not become shared Lounge data, appear with messages, or be published as the identity behind an alias. Other participants should see the alias, not the ledger identity underneath it.

Can an XRPL address ever move through the system?

Yes, when an authorized XRPL action actually requires a destination. The address may move transiently through the encrypted transaction conduit so the transaction can be prepared and signed. That necessary transaction path is different from making the address part of the Lounge identity.

What linkage should Wild Ledger avoid?

Wild Ledger should not create or preserve an unnecessary identity record such as FrogFan ↔ rXXXXXXXX. The temporary alias and the public ledger address serve different purposes and should remain separated wherever possible.

Could the same person use different names in different communities?

Yes. Someone could be SaltyDog in Leaper Lounge and BigBlockBob in Gear Nut Garage. Wild Ledger should not create a public cross-community identity link between those aliases.

Maximize participant anonymity by minimizing identity linkage.

5. What is a Community Manager?

Who is the Community Manager?

The Community Manager is an authorized role in Wild Ledger. For Sailing Frog's Leap, that role is held by the authorized Sailing Frog's Leap manager account. Another community has its own manager or managers.

Why is that important?

Because Wild Ledger should never have special code that means “Rick can do this.” It should understand that an authenticated Community Manager can perform certain actions inside the community they manage.

Can there eventually be more than one manager?

The architecture should allow it. A community might eventually authorize another person to moderate, manage an event, or perform selected community functions without sharing another manager's wallet credentials.

Does typing the manager's nickname give someone manager powers?

No. A manager name can be reserved for appearance, but privileges must come from secure manager authorization. A nickname is never authority.

6. How do arrival and presence work?

Whose arrival is announced?

Every participant. When someone enters a community room, a simple system event appears in that room's current conversation, for example “FrogFan joined Leaper Lounge.” The Community Manager uses the same arrival mechanism as everyone else.

Is a Community Manager arrival treated specially?

No. A scheduled manager appearance may make the arrival important to the community, but the chat engine does not manufacture fanfare or an automatic greeting. If the manager wants to say hello, that is a real message from the manager.

Does the room close when the manager leaves?

No. The room belongs to the community and remains available whether or not a Community Manager is present.

Do we need departure announcements?

No. The design does not require a “left the room” event in the conversation. Live presence can eventually show who is currently connected without cluttering the chat stream with departures.

7. Where does YouTube fit?

Does Wild Ledger need to build its own video streaming system?

Not necessarily. The current concept is that YouTube can provide the broadcast infrastructure while the community's Wild Ledger room provides the participation experience.

YouTube

Video/audio broadcast, bandwidth, distribution, and livestream delivery.

Wild Ledger

Member-gated chat, temporary nicknames, manager presence, recognition, XRP tipping, events, and community history.

Working concept: YouTube is the broadcast infrastructure. The community room is where the community participates.

8. How does value move?

What moves from the Community Manager to a member?

Recognition moves outward. In Sailing Frog's Leap, the manager can recognize participation by issuing LEAP using an XRPL Check. A Great Chat recognition uses the agreed protocol meaning:

SFL-LEAP/BADGE = GREAT-CHAT

The participant's XRPL destination can be supplied through the private transaction path when the award is initiated. The manager should not have to copy and paste a Lounge participant's address, and the alias-to-address relationship should not become shared Lounge data or an unnecessary persistent Wild Ledger identity record.

What moves from a member to the Community Manager?

Appreciation moves inward. A member can voluntarily tip the Community Manager in XRP. The member approves the XRPL transaction from their own wallet; Wild Ledger should not take custody of the member's funds or private keys.

RECOGNITION

Manager → Member

LEAP via XRPL Check

Example meaning: GREAT-CHAT

APPRECIATION

Member → Manager

XRP tip

Voluntary support sent to the Community Manager.

Recognition flows outward in LEAP. Appreciation can flow inward in XRP.

9. What happens when the manager sees a great comment?

What should the interaction feel like?

Fast. The manager taps the member and chooses a standardized recognition such as Great Chat. Wild Ledger initiates the award for that live participant session. The recipient address can move through the encrypted transaction conduit when needed to prepare the XRPL Check and protocol data. The manager then approves the transaction in the wallet and returns to the conversation.

Can the recognition become part of the member's history?

Yes. Once the relevant XRPL transaction is validated and recognized by the Wild Ledger/SFL protocol, that participation can be reflected in the member's Member Record rather than disappearing as a disposable chat moment.

10. Realtime architecture and community isolation

Does the Lounge need a persistent chat database?

No. The Lounge is intentionally a live, ephemeral space rather than a message board. Conversation history does not need to survive after the live experience. Messages, arrival events, and presence are temporary room state.

How do different browsers communicate?

The planned production path is a Cloudflare Worker with Durable Objects and WebSockets. This provides the realtime conduit that lets currently connected browsers exchange room activity without requiring a permanent chat transcript.

Member Record
↓
Community Room in the browser
↓
Encrypted WebSocket connection
↓
Cloudflare Worker + Durable Object realtime room
↓
Other currently connected participants in that Community ID + Room ID

How is community traffic isolated?

Every live connection and room event is scoped by Community ID + Room ID. An SFL browser should never receive ZHR room traffic and merely hide it with JavaScript. The realtime layer should deliver only activity belonging to the authorized community and room.

What is temporary?

Conversation, arrival events, temporary aliases, and live presence are temporary. They exist to support the people who are together in the room now, not to create a permanent social transcript.

11. What should be shared across Wild Ledger?

If communities are isolated, what is the advantage of one platform?

The engine is reusable. Wild Ledger can provide the same underlying capabilities to every participating community while letting each one feel completely different to its members.

Wild Ledger ├── Community │ ├── Manager(s) │ ├── Member Records │ ├── Community Room │ ├── Events │ ├── Challenges │ ├── Badges / Recognition │ └── Community configuration └── Shared XRPL-aware infrastructure

12. What belongs on XRPL?

Does every Lounge interaction belong on the XRP Ledger?

No. Realtime conversation, temporary aliases, arrival events, presence, and similar social activity are intentionally ephemeral and belong in the realtime layer.

What should survive?

XRPL is the durable layer for actions that have lasting meaning: XRP transfers, LEAP delivery, protocol-defined badges, and other community recognition intended to become part of a member's ledger history.

Design principle: Conversation disappears. Recognition survives.

13. The mental model to remember

The community owns the experience.

The temporary nickname controls what people see.
Wild Ledger maximizes participant anonymity by minimizing identity linkage.
The Community Manager is a role, not a hard-coded person.
Communities share infrastructure but not their conversations.
Recognition can flow Manager → Member in the community asset.
Appreciation can flow Member → Manager in XRP.
The realtime room carries the temporary conversation; the XRP Ledger carries durable value and recognition.
Conversation disappears. Recognition survives.

14. What this means for the production path

Do we throw away the Leaper Lounge prototype?

No. Its visible experience remains the foundation. The production version becomes the first configured community implementation of a reusable Wild Ledger realtime room engine.

What is the architectural target?

Keep the website as the community experience, use Cloudflare Worker + Durable Objects/WebSockets as the planned ephemeral realtime layer, and use XRPL for durable value and recognition. Keep Community ID + Room ID isolation, Member Record gating, manager authorization, and alias/XRPL identity separation foundational.

What is deliberately not fixed yet?

Implementation details such as Durable Object naming, heartbeat timing, reconnection rules, and exact presence mechanics remain design work for the production realtime layer. This document records the requirements without pretending those details have already been engineered.
