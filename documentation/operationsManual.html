Foundational Doctrine

Ledger as Authority

Wild Ledger reads and explains canonical state. It does not independently keep canonical state.

Owner + Operator
Canonical state exists only on XRPL. Wild Ledger is an interpreter of canonical state, never its keeper.
Operating Principle

If the website disappeared, the ledger must still contain the facts

Every fact that matters to authoritative Wild Ledger state must be recoverable from validated XRPL history. Wild Ledger may turn those ledger facts into friendly names, statuses, lists, icons, groupings, and workflows, but the screen is an interpretation of the ledger — not a second source of truth.

  • XRPL holds the canonical facts. Membership, recognition, lifecycle state, provenance, and other durable protocol facts must be reconstructible from ledger history.
  • Wild Ledger interprets those facts. Labels such as Available, Active, Retired, Closed, Completed, or Ignored are human-readable interpretations of canonical ledger records.
  • Convenience storage is not authority. Browser storage, caches, configuration files, and temporary drafts may improve the experience, but they must never become the only place an authoritative fact exists.
  • Use the loss test. If losing the website would also lose the fact, the fact has not yet been stored where Wild Ledger requires it to be stored.
Practical standard

A clean Wild Ledger client should be able to rebuild authoritative community state from XRPL and the LEAP Protocol without consulting a conventional application database.

Foundational Doctrine

Human Recognition & Privacy

Wild Ledger should amplify human judgment, not replace it. The Operator remains the human center of recognition.

Owner + Operator
The Operator recognizes. Wild Ledger distributes. XRPL remembers.
Operating Principle

Automate the scaffolding, not the humanity

Wild Ledger is a recognition distribution engine, not an automated scoring system. The Operator decides whether a person did something worth recognizing. Wild Ledger should make that judgment easy to act on, distribute LEAP correctly, preserve the result, and reconstruct the history later.

  • The Operator is the driver. The Operator initiates community recognition and decides what counts. Software may assist with workflow, consistency, validation, and recordkeeping, but it should not invent recognition on the Operator's behalf.
  • Attention is part of participation. Members earn recognition by doing things the Operator chooses to recognize. The community may use comments, chat, direct interaction, in-person meetings, or any other channel the Operator chooses to notice that participation.
  • Recognition is human; the record is durable. The judgment happens in the community. Once the Operator acts, Wild Ledger and XRPL provide the durable scaffolding around that judgment.
Data Minimization

Welcome. We want to know less about you.

Wild Ledger does not need a rich personal profile in order to recognize participation. Ask for no more participant information than is needed to perform the immediate function or interpret LEAP history.

  • Prefer ledger facts to personal profiles. If validated XRPL history already answers the question, do not ask the member to provide the same fact again.
  • An XRPL address is enough for durable LEAP identity. Legal names, email addresses, phone numbers, demographic profiles, interests, and location histories are not prerequisites for ordinary LEAP participation.
  • Keep temporary community identity temporary. A nickname or live-session identifier may be useful for chat or another immediate function, but it should not become a second authoritative member identity.
Business standard

Privacy is not an extra feature layered on later. Knowing less is part of the operating model.

Section 1 · Operating Structure

Wallet Roles & Responsibilities

Wild Ledger uses several XRPL accounts, but they do not all do the same job. The wallet that signs a transaction is part of the protocol meaning.

Owner + Operator
Issuer defines LEAP. Distributor allocates LEAP. Owner authority establishes system-level ownership and first community authority where permitted. The Community Operating Wallet runs the community.
1.1 · The Four Wallet Roles

Know which wallet is doing the job

The same XRPL network and even the same transaction type can be used for different purposes. Wild Ledger determines protocol meaning from the role of the signing account, the transaction, the direction of value, and the canonical memo or state statement.

Asset Identity

LEAP Issuer

The Issuer defines the LEAP asset on XRPL. A member's LEAP trustline points to this issuer. The Issuer is part of LEAP identity; it is not the community's day-to-day operating wallet.

Allocation

Distributor

The Distributor distributes community allocations and replenishments using allocation Checks. Its job is to put operating LEAP into the community side of the system. It does not decide or award member recognition.

Protocol Ownership

Owner Wallet

The Owner Wallet holds protocol-level Owner authority. It may establish the first Community Operating Wallet for a brand-new Community ID and may transfer Owner authority to a successor Owner Wallet. Once a community is established, the Owner Wallet does not take over that community's day-to-day authority.

Community Operations

Community Operating Wallet

The Operating Wallet is the community's on-ledger operating identity. It manages operating LEAP, delivers member recognition by Check or direct Payment, and signs canonical community protocol statements such as recognition lifecycle records.

People, accounts, and signer apps are different things

The Operator is the person or business role running the community. The Community Operating Wallet is the XRPL account authorized to act for that community. Bifrost, Xaman, Tangem, or another compatible wallet app is only a signing tool that controls an XRPL account; changing signer apps does not change the community's protocol authority.

1.2 · Checks

Both Distributor and Operating Wallet use Checks — for different jobs

A Check is an XRPL transaction mechanism. The fact that two roles both use Checks does not make their jobs interchangeable.

Distributor → Allocation Check → Community operating side

An allocation Check makes LEAP available to community operations; cashing the Check completes delivery. It belongs to the allocation and replenishment side of Wild Ledger.

Community Operating Wallet → Recognition Check → Member

A recognition Check offers LEAP to a member for one canonical Badge, Event, or Challenge. The Operating Wallet creates that Check because recognition is a community operation.

Direct recognition is also valid

When a member is already able to receive LEAP, the Community Operating Wallet may deliver recognition immediately by direct Payment. The business meaning is the same: the Operator recognized the member; the transaction is simply a different delivery path.

One mechanism, different protocol meaning

Wild Ledger does not classify a transaction merely because it is a Check. It verifies who signed it, what asset it concerns, where it is going, and which protocol rules apply.

Allocation ownership follows the community namespace

For an allocation such as SFL-LEAP/ALLOCATION=500, the memo says which community owns the allocation. The Check destination says where the Distributor delivered it. If the community later changes its Operating Wallet, the old allocation still belongs to that same community and remains part of its Tokenomics and audit history.

1.3 · Protocol Statements

The Operating Wallet also speaks for the community on XRPL

Not every community action transfers LEAP. When the community needs to publish canonical state, the Operating Wallet can sign a successful validated no-op AccountSet. The transaction moves no LEAP; the public memo is the protocol statement.

  • Community-defined recognition lifecycle. CREATE, END, and IGNORE are no-op AccountSet lifecycle statements for community-defined Badges, Events, and Challenges. They manage whether a recognition opportunity exists and is available; they do not award recognition to a member or move LEAP.
  • Authority state. Single-valued protocol state can use the form <COMMUNITY>-LEAP/AUTHORITY/<KEY>=<VALUE>.
  • No private key leaves the wallet. Wild Ledger constructs and validates the transaction; the wallet app signs it; Wild Ledger submits the signed transaction and verifies the validated result.
The Operating Wallet is more than a LEAP balance

It is the community account whose validated transactions establish community provenance. That is why Wild Ledger must know which Operating Wallet was authorized at the ledger position being interpreted.

1.4 · Operating Wallet State

Changing the Community Operating Wallet

Authority state is expressed as a single current value on XRPL: <COMMUNITY>-LEAP/AUTHORITY/OPERATING-WALLET=<VALUE>. Wild Ledger does not maintain a separate old-wallet/new-wallet history object.

  • Routine replacement is self-service. Once a community has an established canonical Operating Wallet, the currently authorized Operating Wallet may publish a new value directly.
  • Latest valid value wins. The new value becomes authoritative from that validated transaction forward in canonical XRPL transaction order.
  • The ledger is the history. There is no separate BEGIN, END, OLD, NEW, rotation number, or wallet-history database.
  • Historical reporting remains possible. Wild Ledger can determine which Operating Wallet was authoritative at any point by reading the ordered authority-state statements already preserved on XRPL.
Existing-community first statement

For a community that already existed before on-ledger authority state, the already-established Community Operating Wallet publishes one first canonical statement naming itself. That one-time step moves authority state onto XRPL. The Issuer and Distributor do not establish or control the Community Operating Wallet.

Brand-new community provisioning

For a previously unestablished Community ID, the current Owner Wallet publishes the first canonical OPERATING-WALLET value naming that community's first Operating Wallet. After that establishment, later Operating Wallet replacements are controlled by the currently authorized Community Operating Wallet, not by the Owner Wallet.

1.5 · Owner Wallet State

Owner authority can move, but it does not become community authority

The current Owner Wallet may transfer Owner authority to a successor Owner Wallet. That replacement changes who holds Owner authority from that ledger position forward. It does not give the Owner Wallet continuing power to replace an already-established community's Operating Wallet.

Keep the boundary clear

The Owner Wallet can establish the first Operating Wallet for a brand-new Community ID. After establishment, only the currently authorized Community Operating Wallet controls ordinary succession to a new Operating Wallet. The Owner does not seize, recover, or replace that wallet authority.

1.6 · Community Responsibility & Continuity

Wild Ledger does not provide custody or wallet recovery

Under the current operating model, creating and running a Wild Ledger community does not require a fee to Wild Ledger. That does not turn Wild Ledger into a custodian, wallet-recovery service, or guarantor of continued community operation. The Operator is responsible for maintaining access to the Community Operating Wallet and for naming a successor while that wallet can still sign.

  • No key custody. Wild Ledger does not need, hold, or recover the private keys or signing credentials for a Community Operating Wallet.
  • No recovery promise. If the Operator loses access to the current Operating Wallet before validly naming a successor, Wild Ledger does not promise to restore access, replace the wallet, or recreate that authority chain.
  • No Owner takeover. Loss of access does not give the Owner Wallet authority to seize or replace an established community's Operating Wallet.
  • Participation can end. Wild Ledger may stop presenting or supporting a community as active. An Operator does not receive a promise that a community will remain active, listed, supported, or recoverable indefinitely.
  • History remains history. Ending active participation does not rewrite valid XRPL history. Recognition, Earned LEAP, allocations, and other protocol-valid facts that already occurred remain reconstructible from the ledger.
  • Community IDs are not recycled. If an old community can no longer operate, another Operator may be provisioned under a different Community ID and namespace. The old namespace remains part of historical ledger record rather than being reassigned.
Free participation does not imply managed service

Wild Ledger is designed to know less about Operators and members, not more. It does not require a conventional hosted-account relationship in order to establish community authority on XRPL, and it does not assume responsibility for an Operator's wallet access merely because the community uses Wild Ledger.

Protocol boundary. LEAP Protocol 0.20 does not yet define a canonical on-ledger Owner transaction that permanently deactivates an established community. Until the Protocol defines that rule, a Wild Ledger decision to stop listing, presenting, or supporting a community as active is an operating decision and must not be misrepresented as canonical LEAP Protocol state.
Wild Ledger does not recover communities and does not seize communities. An Operator is responsible for the wallet that carries the community's authority.
Section 2 · Recognition Operations

Recognition Operations

Recognition is the human center of Wild Ledger. The Operator creates the opportunity, notices the participation, makes the judgment, and chooses when to recognize it.

Owner + Operator
Show up. Do the thing. Make a difference.
2.1 · The Three Recognition Types

Use the category that matches the human story

Event

Show up.

An Event says, “I was there.” It marks a specific real-world occurrence where the member and the community crossed paths. A member earns a particular Event once. When the occurrence is over, the Operator ends it.

Badge

Do the thing.

A Badge says, “I did that.” It recognizes a repeatable act, contribution, skill, role, shared moment, or other behavior the Operator values. The same Badge may be awarded to the same member more than once while it remains active.

Challenge

Make a difference.

A Challenge recognizes completion of a defined objective. It is useful when the community asks members to take on something meaningful — for example helping a cause, completing a project, or improving a place — and the Operator decides the objective was completed. A member earns a particular Challenge ID once.

Great Chat is the reference repeatable Badge

Great Chat is available to every established LEAP community. The protocol supplies the Badge; the Operator supplies the judgment. There is no automatic engagement score. The Operator decides when a contribution deserves Great Chat and how much LEAP to award within the protocol rules.

2.2 · Operator Judgment

The Operator recognizes

Nothing about ordinary recognition requires a machine to decide that a member earned it. The Operator may notice participation anywhere the community actually lives: an existing content channel, community chat, a comment thread, a direct interaction, or an in-person meeting.

  • Do not automate worthiness. Software can organize opportunities and prepare the distribution, but the Operator decides whether the contribution counts.
  • Do not force a single intake channel. A community should not need a formal claim form for every human interaction. The Operator can recognize what the Operator genuinely sees.
  • LEAP is recognition, not an objective performance score. Award amounts express the Operator's recognition within the community; they should not be presented as a machine-generated measurement of a person's value.
2.3 · Opportunity vs. History

Public recognition asks “What can I do?” Member history answers “What have I done?”

Keep opportunity and history separate. A public recognition surface should help people discover what they can participate in now. A member record should show what that member actually earned.

Use recognition to pull people into participation

The opportunity should be clear enough to create curiosity and action without trying to replace the creator, organization's existing content, or community channels. The story can live where the audience already is; Wild Ledger gives that participation structure and memory.

Lifecycle manages the menu. Recognition records what a member earned.

These are two different XRPL jobs. A lifecycle statement says whether a community-defined Badge, Event, or Challenge exists as an opportunity. A member-recognition transaction says that a particular member actually earned one. Creating the opportunity does not recognize a member, and recognizing a member does not create the definition.

2.4 · Create, End, Ignore

Manage the life of a community-defined recognition opportunity

Think of CREATE, END, and IGNORE as lifecycle flags for the community's recognition menu. They are successful validated no-op AccountSet transactions: the memo changes canonical lifecycle state, but the transaction does not transfer LEAP and does not say that any member earned the recognition.

Example: a July developers meetup

The community can publish CREATE for a recognition such as DEVELOPERS-MEETUP-JULY. That puts the opportunity on the board. If Alice attends and the Operator decides she earned it, the Operating Wallet later creates a separate recognition transaction for Alice. When the meetup is over, END stops future use of that recognition without erasing Alice's earned history.

Great Chat shows the difference

GREAT-CHAT is already defined by the LEAP Protocol, so a community never lifecycle-CREATEs the Great Chat definition. The Operator still creates each individual Great Chat recognition transaction when a member earns it. The Protocol defines what Great Chat is; the Operator creates the member's recognition record.

  • CREATE establishes a legitimate community-defined recognition. Create it when the community genuinely intends to use that Badge, Event, or Challenge.
  • END means retire it. Future use stops, but legitimate recognition already earned remains part of the member's history.
  • IGNORE means quarantine it. Use IGNORE for an erroneous, test, invalid, or otherwise non-operational definition. It is removed from normal recognition presentation and history, but the underlying XRPL facts remain.
  • IGNORE does not claw back LEAP. LEAP already delivered by otherwise valid recognition remains Earned LEAP and continues to count toward rank.
  • END and IGNORE are final. There is no reopen, un-end, or un-ignore. Once a community-defined recognition ID has been established, that ID remains permanently reserved and is never reused, whether it is later ended or ignored.
IGNORE is not a cleanup shortcut. Use it when the definition itself was a mistake. Use END when the recognition was legitimate and simply reached the end of its useful life.
2.5 · When Recognition Counts

Recognition counts when LEAP is actually delivered

The Operator makes the judgment, but the ledger determines when the award becomes part of the member's Earned LEAP history. The timing depends on how the recognition is delivered.

Payment

Earned when delivered.

A protocol-valid recognition Payment counts when the Payment succeeds in validated XRPL history and actually delivers the LEAP to the member.

Check

Earned when cashed.

CheckCreate is the recognition offer. It does not add Earned LEAP by itself. The recognition counts when the member successfully cashes the Check and the LEAP is actually delivered.

Award Rule

One award. One reason.

Each recognition award records exactly one Badge, Event, or Challenge reason. The award may deliver one or more LEAP, but the amount must be a positive whole number.

Remember the repetition rule

Badges may repeat. A member may earn the same Badge more than once. A particular Event ID counts once for that member, and a particular Challenge ID counts once for that member.

Payment = earned when delivered. Check = earned when cashed. One recognition award. One reason.
2.6 · Recognition vs. Spread

Two questions, one ledger

Wild Ledger reads the same validated XRPL history through two different lenses. Recognition asks what a member earned. Spread Score asks how broadly the community spread LEAP. The same transaction can sometimes answer both questions, but the two records do not mean the same thing.

Member View

What did this member earn?

This belongs in the member record. Only protocol-valid recognition adds Earned LEAP and recognition history. That history can include Badges, Events, Challenges, Great Chat, and the Earned LEAP used to reconstruct rank.

Community View

How far have we spread?

This belongs in the community and Operator view. Spread Score measures qualifying outward LEAP distribution under Wild Ledger operating policy and helps determine when the community earns more operating inventory.

One transaction can answer both questions

If Alice receives 10 LEAP through a protocol-valid Great Chat recognition, Alice's member record gains 10 Earned LEAP. If Alice is also a qualifying outside wallet, that same outward distribution can contribute up to 10 points to the community's Spread Score.

A LEAP transfer is not automatically recognition

If Bob receives 10 LEAP in an ordinary transfer that does not satisfy the LEAP Protocol's recognition rules, the transfer may still contribute to Spread Score if Bob is a qualifying outside wallet. But Bob did not earn a Badge, Event, Challenge, Great Chat, Earned LEAP, or rank credit from that transfer.

Spread Score is a Wild Ledger metric

Spread Score is calculated from validated XRPL history, but it is not LEAP, a token, an award, a rank, or canonical LEAP Protocol state. It is Wild Ledger's operating measure of community reach.

Member side: What have I earned? Community side: How far have we spread?
2.7 · The Participation Flywheel

Recognition should strengthen the community that already exists

Wild Ledger works best when it amplifies an existing creator, organization, or community rather than trying to replace its audience channels.

Story or content → Recognition opportunity → Community interaction → Operator recognition → LEAP distribution → Member history → More participation
  • Let the main channel tell the story. Videos, posts, live streams, meetings, or other community content can explain the context and create interest.
  • Let recognition create the invitation. The recognition surface shows that there are real things to show up for, do, and contribute to.
  • Let community interaction create attention. Chat, comments, and real-world interaction give members ways to get the Operator's attention through genuine participation.
  • Let the ledger preserve the result. After the Operator recognizes the member, the history becomes durable and can continue to tell that member's story later.
Section 3 · Token Operations

Replenishment Strategy

Wild Ledger replenishment rewards communities that spread LEAP outward. The policy does not tell an Operator how much LEAP to give any one person. It defines how a community earns more operating inventory from Wild Ledger.

Owner + Operator
You are free to distribute LEAP however you want. But the way you earn more inventory from Wild Ledger is by spreading LEAP to more wallets.
3.1 · The Business Rule

Spread LEAP to earn more LEAP

Each community begins with an initial operating allocation of 1,000 LEAP. Wild Ledger then measures how broadly that community distributes LEAP from its authorized Community Operating Wallet history.

The simple version

Your community starts with 1,000 LEAP. You can give LEAP however you want. Each outside wallet can add up to 20 lifetime points to your Spread Score. Every 500 points earns your community another 1,000 LEAP. Giving one wallet more than 20 LEAP is fine. It just will not add more than 20 points. That wallet's 20-point cap never resets. Sending LEAP back to yourself does not help.

Read the score as progress, not recognition

A display such as Spread Score: 327 / 500 means the community has 327 qualifying points toward its next 1,000 LEAP replenishment. It does not mean that any member earned 327 LEAP. Member recognition belongs in the member record; Spread Score belongs in the community's operating view.

The incentive is intentional

Wild Ledger does not reward a community for simply holding LEAP. It rewards successful outward distribution. If a community spreads LEAP well, that community earns more operating inventory and has more LEAP available to keep participating and growing.

3.2 · Spread Score

One wallet can help — but only up to 20

Each qualifying recipient wallet contributes its net LEAP received, capped at 20, to the community's lifetime Spread Score. That 20-point lifetime cap never resets. Spread Score is derived from validated XRPL history, but it is a Wild Ledger operating metric rather than LEAP Protocol state. This lets an Operator keep distributing LEAP to the same person without letting one wallet drive replenishment forever.

1 · Net Distribution to Wallet w
Nw = max(0, Sentw − Returnedw)

Sentw is LEAP sent from the community's authorized Operating Wallet history to recipient wallet w. Returnedw is LEAP later sent from that recipient wallet back to the community's authorized Operating Wallet history.

2 · Wallet Credit
Cw = min(20, Nw)

A recipient wallet can contribute no more than 20 points to Spread Score over its lifetime. Once that wallet reaches 20 points, it can never add another Spread Score point for that community. The Operator may still distribute more LEAP to that wallet; the extra amount simply does not increase replenishment credit.

3 · Spread Score
S = Σ Cw

The community's Spread Score is the sum of the wallet credits for all qualifying recipient wallets.

4 · Total Replenishment Earned
E = 1000 × floor(S ÷ 500)

Every full 500 points of lifetime Spread Score earns the community another 1,000 LEAP of replenishment.

5 · Replenishment Available Now
A = max(0, E − Paid)

Paid is the total replenishment Wild Ledger has already delivered to that community, not including the initial 1,000 LEAP allocation. This prevents the same Spread Score from earning twice.

3.3 · What Counts

Real outward distribution counts

  • Count successful, validated LEAP transfers from the community's authorized Operating Wallet history to qualifying external recipient wallets.
  • Use the full authorized Operating Wallet history. Changing the community's Operating Wallet does not reset Spread Score. The lifetime wallet credits do not reset either.
  • Do not count internal or controlled wallets. The Issuer, Distributor, Owner Wallet, the community's own Operating Wallets, and other wallets controlled by the Operator or community do not qualify as outside recipients.
  • Subtract LEAP that comes back. If a recipient sends LEAP back to the community's authorized Operating Wallet history, that return reduces the recipient wallet's net contribution.
  • No clawback of replenishment already delivered. If later returns reduce Spread Score below a prior threshold, Wild Ledger does not take back LEAP already replenished. The community must rebuild the score before another replenishment becomes available.
Do not manufacture spread. Creating or using wallets you control, moving LEAP in circles, or routing LEAP through related wallets does not create qualifying distribution. Wild Ledger may exclude transfers that are reasonably identified as self-dealing or circular movement.
3.4 · Examples

What the formula rewards

500 LEAP to one wallet

The Operator may make that distribution, but one wallet can contribute only 20 points.

Cw = min(20, 500) = 20
S = 20
E = 1000 × floor(20 ÷ 500) = 0
0 LEAP earned

20 LEAP to 25 wallets

Twenty-five different qualifying wallets each reach the 20-point wallet cap.

S = 25 × 20 = 500
E = 1000 × floor(500 ÷ 500) = 1000
1,000 LEAP earned
What happens next?

Those same 25 wallets may continue receiving LEAP, but each has already contributed its full 20 lifetime points. To move Spread Score from 500 to 1,000, the community must create more qualifying wallet reach. The old wallets do not reset and cannot be reused for another 20 points. Once Spread Score reaches 1,000, total replenishment earned becomes 2,000 LEAP. If 1,000 has already been delivered, another 1,000 becomes available.

3.5 · Operator Playbook

Keep it simple

  • Start with 1,000 LEAP.
  • Use LEAP however your community needs.
  • Spread LEAP outward if you want Wild Ledger to give the community more inventory.
  • Remember the 20-point lifetime cap per qualifying recipient wallet. It never resets.
  • Every 500 Spread Score points earns another 1,000 LEAP.
  • Do not move LEAP through wallets you control just to increase the score.
If your community spreads LEAP successfully, your community wins and Wild Ledger wins.
