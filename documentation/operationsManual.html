Section 1 · Token Operations

Replenishment Strategy

Wild Ledger replenishment is designed to support communities that put LEAP into circulation. This section explains what the algorithm measures, why broad circulation matters, and how to decide when a replenishment request makes sense.

Owner + Operator
Circulate first. Replenish second. Grow through participation.
1.1 · Replenishment Cycles

Think in cycles, not calendar months

A replenishment is an economic event, not a monthly entitlement. Every completed replenishment closes one cycle and starts the next. The next calculation uses only activity that happens after that new starting line.

1

Start the cycle

For the first cycle, measurement begins with the community's initial operating allocation. After that, the timestamp of the last completed replenishment becomes the new starting line.

2

Deploy LEAP

Wild Ledger measures qualifying LEAP distributed from the Community Operating Wallet during the current cycle.

3

Measure circulation

The algorithm looks at how concentrated that cycle's distribution is. Broad distribution counts more; concentration counts less.

4

Replenish and reset

When replenishment is completed, the old cycle is closed. Its deployment cannot qualify again. New activity starts building the next cycle from zero.

The rule that prevents double counting

Deployment, circulation, Effective Deployment, and Replenishment are calculated from the most recent completed replenishment forward. The same deployment can never earn replenishment twice.

1.2 · Replenishment Algorithm

How the Wild Ledger algorithm works

The algorithm deliberately separates movement from healthy circulation. Moving LEAP out of the operating wallet matters, but concentrating it in one or a few wallets should not produce the same replenishment as broad community participation.

1 · Deployment Ratio
DR = D ÷ (D + I)

D is qualifying LEAP distributed during the current cycle. I is current LEAP inventory in the Community Operating Wallet. DR shows how much operating supply has moved out.

2 · Concentration
C = Σ(sᵢ²)

Each sᵢ is one recipient wallet's share of the current cycle's qualifying distribution. A small number of dominant wallets pushes concentration higher.

3 · Circulation Factor
CF = 1 − C

One wallet holding the whole cycle produces a factor near zero. As distribution becomes broader, the factor moves toward one.

4 · Circulation Ratio
CR = DR × CF

This is Deployment Ratio after concentration is taken into account. It is the dashboard's anti-hoarding signal.

5 · Effective Deployment
ED = D × CF

This converts circulation quality back into a LEAP amount. Effective Deployment is the amount the replenishment policy recognizes.

6 · Replenishment
R = ED × 1.03

Under the current 3% Growth Rate Provision, Wild Ledger replaces qualifying Effective Deployment and adds 3% additional LEAP liquidity for community growth.

In plain language

Wild Ledger asks two questions: How much LEAP did you put into the community? and How broadly did it circulate? Replenishment grows when both answers improve.

1.3 · Timing

Why waiting can improve the result

The Replenishment card turning green means a request is available. It does not mean the operator should immediately request it. Because a completed replenishment closes the current cycle, requesting too early can reduce what the cycle ultimately produces.

One stronger cycle

Suppose the community distributes 100 LEAP equally among 10 recipients before requesting.

CF = 1 − 10(0.10²) = 0.90
ED = 100 × 0.90 = 90
R = 90 × 1.03 = 92.7
92.7 LEAP

Broad circulation is rewarded before the cycle closes.

Two early requests

Now split the same 100 LEAP into two cycles of 50 LEAP, each spread equally across only 5 recipients.

Each cycle: CF = 0.80
R = 50 × 0.80 × 1.03 = 41.2
Two cycles = 82.4
82.4 LEAP

Closing the first cycle early leaves less circulation inside each individual cycle.

The incentive is intentional

The operator has a reason to keep distributing and broaden participation before requesting replenishment. The system does not need to order an operator to distribute a specific percentage. The economics encourage it.

1.4 · Decision Standard

When should you request?

Before requesting replenishment, ask:

  • Have I distributed enough of this cycle's LEAP to make replenishment meaningful?
  • Is circulation broadening? If the same few wallets are absorbing most distributions, another round of participation may improve the cycle more than an immediate request.
  • Do I still have enough operating inventory? Waiting is useful only while the community can continue operating normally.
  • Would more real community activity improve this cycle? A green card means you may request; it does not create a deadline.

Use the dashboard as a decision tool

Deployment Ratio shows movement. Circulation Ratio shows quality of circulation. Effective Deployment shows how much distribution counts. Replenishment shows what the current cycle can support under the 3% GRP.

Green = Available
Do not manufacture deployment. Moving LEAP into one or a few controlled or concentrated wallets is exactly what the concentration adjustment is designed to discount. Replenishment should follow real participation, not wallet shuffling.
1.5 · Quick Reference

The operator playbook

  • Start with operating inventory.
  • Use LEAP to recognize genuine participation.
  • Distribute broadly rather than concentrating awards.
  • Watch Effective Deployment, not raw distribution alone.
  • Let a strong cycle develop before requesting, unless operating inventory requires action sooner.
  • Request replenishment when the cycle makes economic sense.
  • After completion, begin again from the new replenishment timestamp.
LEAP grows when LEAP circulates.
