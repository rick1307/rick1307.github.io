Leaper Lounge • Discord Integration

Leaper Lounge

The logbook can remember who is in the crew, where they stand now, and what they have done. Leaper Lounge gives that state a home. Its Discord server is Leaper Lounge — the social side of the experience, where conversation, access, recognition, events, challenges, and community come together.

The goal is not to move LEAP into Discord. The goal is to let Leaper Lounge read the LEAP Logbook and turn that state into a living crew experience.

01

Give Discord one job.

The Leaper Lounge Discord server is the social side of the broader Leaper Lounge experience. It is not the ledger.

The XRP Ledger remains the durable record underneath LEAP. Discord should consume that record and make it useful to people. If Discord disappears someday, the member's LEAP balance and permanent logbook history should still exist.

That separation keeps the architecture clean: LEAP remembers. Discord responds.

Current state

Membership / Access

Leaper Lounge can recognize a verified LEAP holder and open the social spaces intended for crew.

Current state

Rank

Deckhand through Master of the Leap can become a current Discord role that changes as LEAP holdings change.

Permanent history

Badges + Challenges + Events

These belong in the Logbook. Discord should display them when useful rather than turning every historical fact into a role.

Open Clubhouse, Verified Crew

Leaper Lounge is the open clubhouse. LEAP is the crew credential.

People do not need LEAP to enter Leaper Lounge. The server can remain discoverable and welcoming to anyone who wants to follow the community.

LEAP verification changes what a member can do, not whether they are allowed through the door. A verified member can receive a visible current rank, participate in LEAP challenges, earn badges and recognition, and build permanent Crew Record history.

Unverified members can still be part of the community, see challenges unfold, talk with the crew, and decide later whether they want to join the LEAP system. Challenge spaces can remain visible so newcomers can see the game being played; LEAP-specific submissions, claims, awards, rank progression, and Logbook history belong to verified Leapers.

This creates discovery naturally: someone can enter the clubhouse first, see the game being played, and decide later that they want to become part of the crew.

Discord membership is open. LEAP participation is verified.

02

Keep the server small and obvious.

Three permanent text rooms and two voice spaces are enough. Use threads for specific conversations instead of multiplying channels.

Mess Deck

Open to everyone, including members with no LEAP rank. Anyone can read, post, reply, react, and ask questions. Members with no LEAP rank cannot start new threads.

Chief's Mess

Open to Bosun and above. Bosuns can read, post, reply, and participate in existing threads. Quartermaster and above can also start new threads here.

Ward Room

Open to First Mate and above. First Mates can read, post, reply, and participate in existing threads. Master of the Leap can also start new threads here.

Crew Voice

Open conversation for everyone. Ranked and unranked members can join and talk without the LEAP hierarchy controlling ordinary voice participation.

Live Stage

The dedicated place for Rick's live sessions. Members always know where a live event will happen, while Rick controls the stage and who is speaking.

Thread rule: Deckhand unlocks thread creation in the Mess Deck. Quartermaster unlocks thread creation in the Chief's Mess. Master of the Leap unlocks thread creation in the Ward Room. Privileges are cumulative, and owner/admin permissions remain separate from LEAP rank.
03

Use Discord's native tools first.

Do not install a bot for something Discord already does well.

A Community server can use native Onboarding and Server Guide to orient new members, AutoMod to handle basic moderation, Scheduled Events for live gatherings, polls for quick decisions, and ordinary role/channel permissions for access control.

Native

Onboarding

Show only the most useful channels first and let members choose optional interests.

Native

Server Guide

Turn the welcome experience into a simple explanation of the crew, LEAP, and where to begin.

Native

AutoMod

Block spam, risky links, mass mentions, and obvious abuse without making moderators chase everything manually.

Native

Events + Polls

Schedule live crew moments and ask the community questions without adding more infrastructure.

Practical note: Discord currently lets members experience Onboarding on any device, but admins configure Onboarding on desktop. Linked Roles are also currently an admin-desktop feature. That is worth knowing before we design a workflow around an iPhone-only setup.
04

Make rank visible without turning it into authority.

Discord keeps exactly one current LEAP rank role. Having a rank already implies LEAP verification; there is no separate “LEAP Verified” role.

No LEAP rankMess Deck access: read, post, reply, react, and join existing threads. Cannot create new threads. No access to Chief's Mess or Ward Room.
DeckhandEverything above, plus the ability to create threads in the Mess Deck.
BosunEverything above, plus access to Chief's Mess. Can read, post, reply, and join existing threads there.
QuartermasterEverything above, plus the ability to create threads in Chief's Mess.
First MateEverything above, plus access to the Ward Room. Can read, post, reply, and join existing threads there.
Master of the LeapEverything above, plus the ability to create threads in the Ward Room.

Privileges are cumulative. Each rank adds one clear piece of access or conversation-shaping ability.

Rank is status, not authority. LEAP rank never grants moderation, kick/ban, Manage Roles, Manage Server, or other administrative powers. Moderator/admin roles remain completely separate.

Discord remembers status, not holdings. The Worker may temporarily read the XRPL address and LEAP balance to calculate rank, but the Discord integration retains only the resulting rank role. It does not retain the LEAP amount or a permanent wallet-to-Discord identity mapping.

Reverification replaces rank. When a member verifies again, the Worker removes any old LEAP rank role and assigns the current one. A member should never accumulate multiple LEAP rank roles.

The baseline can remain nearly bot-free. Add a third-party bot only when it solves a real problem.

06

Use the bridge to assign the current rank.

The bridge already proves the member's current LEAP state. The next job is to turn that result into exactly one Discord rank role.

The verification + role flow

A member starts from their verified Crew Record and authorizes Discord. During that handoff, the Worker temporarily has the Discord user identity and the XRPL account context at the same time. It re-checks the current LEAP balance, calculates the current rank, assigns exactly one Discord rank role, clears the temporary bridge data, and redirects the member into Leaper Lounge.

The handoff is temporary by design. The Discord integration does not keep a wallet-to-Discord lookup table and does not store the member's LEAP balance. The only lasting LEAP state in Discord is the current rank role.

/verify
Start a fresh LEAP verification and refresh the member's current Discord rank.
/rank
Show the member's current Discord LEAP rank without exposing or storing the LEAP balance.
/crew-record
Open the Crew Record experience on the website rather than turning Discord into the permanent history store.
/challenge
Show the current LEAP challenge and how to participate.
/badges
Point members to their badge history in the Crew Record instead of creating badge roles.
/resync
Run fresh verification when a member wants Discord to recalculate and replace the current rank role.
Bridge

Discord identity

OAuth identifies the Discord member for the current verification callback.

Bridge

Temporary account context

The verified XRPL account is carried through the signed bridge only long enough to complete the check.

XRPL

Balance check

The Worker reads the current LEAP balance from public XRPL data and uses it as an input, not stored Discord data.

Rules

Rank engine

Current LEAP balance resolves to exactly one rank: Deckhand, Bosun, Quartermaster, First Mate, or Master of the Leap.

Privacy

Discard the bridge

After role assignment, the temporary wallet/Discord pairing and balance are not retained by the integration.

Discord

Role sync

Remove any old LEAP rank role, assign the current one, then let Discord's native channel permissions enforce the room and thread rules.

07

Let the Logbook drive engagement.

The integration becomes valuable when it creates a repeatable loop, not when it merely proves that a wallet can talk to Discord.

1. EpisodeA new Sailing Frog's Leap episode creates the moment.
2. ChallengeA clear action gives viewers a reason to participate.
3. CommunityLeaper Lounge gives the challenge a place to live and people a place to react.
4. RecognitionLEAP or a permanent record recognizes the participation.
5. LogbookThe member's history becomes cumulative rather than disposable.
6. ReturnThe next episode arrives with history, rank, and identity already attached.
This is where the phrase “turn my adventure into their game” becomes software. Leaper Lounge provides the place to play. The Logbook makes participation persist.
08

Build it in phases.

Each phase should work on its own. Automation comes only after the behavior is worth automating.

Phase 0 — Now

Learn how people use Leaper Lounge.

Create the server, roles, simple channels, rules, voice room, and a place for episode discussion. Invite a few trusted people. Learn the rhythm before adding machinery.

Phase 1 — Native foundation

Turn on the community features that reduce friction.

Community mode, Onboarding, Server Guide, AutoMod, Scheduled Events, and simple polls. Make joining understandable and moderation boring.

Phase 2 — Manual LEAP experience

Test the product before writing the bridge.

Manually recognize Deckhands, run a challenge, post an event, celebrate a badge, and see whether people care. This proves the social behavior before software hardens it.

Phase 3 — LEAP app

Connect Discord identity to the public Logbook.

Add verification, rank sync, /logbook, /rank, /challenge, and a clean way to refresh state. Prefer slash commands and buttons so the app does not need to read ordinary conversation.

Phase 4 — Automation

Let important state changes create community moments.

New badge earned? Recognition post. Rank changed? Role updates. New challenge? Discord announces it. Event recorded? It appears in the member's Logbook. Automate only the things that proved valuable manually.

Phase 5 — The big finish

Leaper Lounge becomes a live cockpit for the LEAP Logbook.

A member can enter Leaper Lounge, join the Leaper Lounge Discord server, verify once, see current rank, open their permanent history, find the current challenge, join events, participate in polls, and receive recognition — while the source of truth remains outside Discord on the ledger.

The finished experience should feel simple.

A Leaper should not feel like they are operating blockchain software. They should feel like they joined a crew, participated in an adventure, earned recognition, and built a history that follows them forward.

09

Protect the architecture from clutter.

A few things are worth deciding now so the project does not become harder later.

Do not make Discord the source of truth.The ledger is the durable state. Discord is an interface.
Do not make every badge a role.Ranks are current status. Badges, events, and completed challenges are history.
Do not ask for wallet secrets.Verification should prove control with a signature. Public addresses and public ledger data are enough afterward.
Do not over-gate the community.Rank should add recognition and occasional access, not make a Deckhand feel like a second-class member.
Do not install bots because they are popular.Every bot adds permissions, complexity, and another service dependency.
Do not make message surveillance the integration.Use slash commands, buttons, explicit events, and ledger state wherever possible.
