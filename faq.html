LEAP Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ

You do not need to be a crypto expert to join the Sailing Frog's Leap crew. Here are simple answers to the questions new crew members are most likely to have.

Getting started

The basics

Do I have to buy LEAP? No. LEAP is a crew membership and recognition token. When you earn LEAP, Sailing Frog's Leap can send it to you by Check.
Can I transfer LEAP to someone else? Yes. Checks are how Sailing Frog's Leap distributes LEAP from the distributor. Once you cash a Check, that LEAP is in your wallet and the Check has done its job. If transfers are enabled, you can send LEAP directly to another LEAP holder — keep it, share it with another crew member, or give some to someone new and help grow the community.
Is XRP the same as LEAP? No. XRP is the native asset of the XRP Ledger. LEAP is the Sailing Frog's Leap crew token that lives on the XRP Ledger.
Wallet, privacy & cost

Why LEAP uses an XRPL address

Your public address is not your wallet secret.

LEAP only needs the public XRP Ledger address you choose to use for your Crew Record. I never need your recovery words, private key, family seed, or recovery phrase.

For privacy, I recommend using a separate XRPL wallet for LEAP instead of your primary crypto wallet.

Why does LEAP need my XRP Ledger address? Your public XRPL address is how LEAP knows which Crew Record is yours. Think of it as the number on your membership passport. LEAP uses that address to find your LEAP balance, rank, badges, and history on the public XRP Ledger. You do not create a separate LEAP username or password.
Is my XRPL address private? No. An XRPL address and the transactions associated with it are public on the XRP Ledger. That is an important thing to understand before you join.
If I give LEAP my XRPL address, can you see what that wallet has done? Yes. The XRP Ledger is public, so anyone who knows an XRPL address can view the public activity associated with that address. I want to be straight about that. It is also why I recommend using a separate wallet for LEAP. LEAP does not need to know anything about your other crypto activity.
Are you building a database that connects my identity to my XRPL address? LEAP is not designed around a private identity database. The Join page currently asks you for one thing: the public XRPL address you choose to use with LEAP. It does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, home address, or legal identity. That Join request is sent through the site's form service so your first LEAP Check can be processed. Your Crew Record itself is built from public XRP Ledger data, not from a private LEAP database of names and balances.
What personal information does LEAP actually need from me? To join, the website asks for the public XRPL address you choose to use with LEAP. I do not need your private key, recovery phrase, banking information, government ID, or the address of your primary crypto wallet.
Why do you recommend a separate wallet for LEAP? Because that is what I would do myself. A public blockchain address can reveal its public transaction history, and LEAP does not need to know anything about your other crypto activity. A dedicated LEAP wallet creates a clean boundary: your LEAP membership activity stays in one wallet, and everything else stays somewhere else.
Can Sailing Frog's Leap access or control my wallet? No. Knowing your public XRPL address does not give me control of your wallet. You control it with your private keys. Never send me — or anyone else — your recovery words, private key, family seed, or recovery phrase.
Do I have to put a lot of XRP in this wallet? No. XRP pays the XRP Ledger's very small transaction fees and supports its reserve requirements. The XRP Ledger currently requires a 1 XRP base reserve, and some ledger objects can increase the reserve requirement. For a simple dedicated LEAP wallet, a few XRP is enough under today's requirements. Reserve requirements can change, and XRP held for reserve is not a LEAP membership fee.
Why do you recommend Xaman? Xaman is currently the wallet I recommend because it gives new crew members a clear path through the XRP Ledger features LEAP uses, including enabling LEAP and cashing LEAP Checks. Supporting one wallet first also lets me give everyone the same simple instructions.
Do I have to use Xaman? LEAP is built on the XRP Ledger, not on Xaman. Your Crew Record is tied to your XRPL address. Xaman is simply the wallet I currently support for the full LEAP process. Other XRPL wallets can be supported as I verify that they handle the LEAP steps cleanly.
Why not just use an email address and password? Because LEAP is designed so the XRP Ledger itself is the permanent logbook. Your public XRPL address connects you to your Crew Record without requiring a separate LEAP username and password system. The website is the front door, LEAP is the passport, and the XRP Ledger is the logbook.
LEAP Checks

About the Checks

What is a LEAP Check? A LEAP Check is a payment feature built into the XRP Ledger. I use it to offer you LEAP without simply pushing tokens into your wallet. You choose whether to cash the Check and accept the LEAP.
Why use Checks instead of just sending LEAP? Checks let you choose whether to accept each LEAP award. They also give each award its own record on the XRP Ledger. Once you cash the Check, the LEAP is in your wallet and can move like any other LEAP.
Do I need to set up LEAP before I receive a Check? No separate LEAP setup is required before I send the Check. Xaman guides you through what is needed when you cash it.
Do I have to cash a Check? No. It is your wallet. A pending Check is an offer of LEAP. You choose whether to cash it and accept the LEAP.
What happens after a LEAP Check is created? A Check can be waiting, cashed, or canceled. While it is waiting, it appears as LEAP Waiting for You. If you cash it, the LEAP reaches your wallet and the recognition can become part of your Crew Record. If the Check is canceled, it is no longer available to cash and does not count as an earned recognition in your Crew Record.
Can a LEAP Check record why I earned it? Yes. When I create a LEAP Check, I can use the CheckCreate transaction's standard XRP Ledger Memos field to record the recognition. LEAP uses entries such as SFL-LEAP/BADGE=NEW-MEMBER, SFL-LEAP/EVENT=LA-CRUZ-BEACH-BONFIRE, or SFL-LEAP/CHALLENGE=LEAP-001.
Crew Record

Your LEAP record

What does my Crew Record show? Your Crew Record shows your current LEAP balance and rank, any outstanding LEAP Checks waiting for you, and the LEAP history you have actually earned on the XRP Ledger.
What is LEAP Waiting for You? It is the list of LEAP Checks that are currently available for you to cash. A Check stays there while it is outstanding and disappears after it is cashed or canceled.
When does something become part of my Crew Record? A Check-based recognition becomes part of your personal Crew Record after that Check is successfully cashed. A Check that is still waiting or that was canceled does not count as earned.
What happens to old canceled Checks? They remain part of the public XRP Ledger transaction history, but they are not shown as earned badges or recognitions in your personal Crew Record.
Badges

How badges work

What is a badge? A badge is a permanent crew recognition for something meaningful: a milestone, a contribution, a special status, a voyage, a place, or another part of your story.
Does rank control which badges I can have? No. Rank is based on how much LEAP you currently hold, while badges record your history. A Deckhand can have a special badge that a higher-ranking member does not.
How is a badge recorded? Sailing Frog's Leap can offer a badge through a LEAP Check with a standardized memo naming the recognition. When the recipient cashes that Check, the badge becomes part of that member's earned Crew Record.
Does a badge have to add more LEAP? No. The LEAP amount can be small and symbolic. The important part of the badge can be the recognition and the ledger record that documents it.
What does the badge memo do? The memo gives the recognition a consistent name inside the LEAP namespace, such as SFL-LEAP/BADGE=NEW-MEMBER or SFL-LEAP/BADGE=LEAP-OG, so the badge can be identified later from the ledger history.
Does the badge record stay on the XRP Ledger? Yes. The underlying transactions remain part of the public XRP Ledger history. The Crew Record interprets that history and shows the badges the member has actually earned.
How are badges shown in my Crew Record? Your Crew Record lists each earned badge once. Badge dates are not the point of the personal record; the important thing is that the recognition is part of your crew history.
Who decides when a badge is earned? During the pilot, Sailing Frog's Leap defines the badge rules and awards recognition. If the system grows, some recognitions could later be automated.
Technical details

Under the hood

You do not need to understand any of this to use LEAP. This section is for anyone interested in how LEAP uses the XRP Ledger under the hood.

Memos & Transactions

Where does the memo actually live? The memo belongs to the CheckCreate transaction that creates the Check. It does not belong to an individual LEAP token or to the Check object itself. Cashing uses a separate CheckCash transaction, and canceling uses a separate CheckCancel transaction. The original CheckCreate and its memo remain in public ledger history either way.
Does the memo travel with one specific LEAP coin? No. LEAP is fungible. The memo belongs to the transaction record, not to one individually serialized LEAP unit.

LEAP Naming Convention

What is the LEAP memo format? LEAP uses the namespace SFL-LEAP followed by a category and value: SFL-LEAP/<CATEGORY>=<VALUE>.
What categories does LEAP use? The current categories are BADGE, EVENT, and CHALLENGE. Examples include SFL-LEAP/BADGE=LEAP-OG, SFL-LEAP/EVENT=LA-CRUZ-BEACH-BONFIRE, and SFL-LEAP/CHALLENGE=LEAP-001.
Does the XRP Ledger understand what SFL-LEAP means? No. The XRP Ledger stores the memo data. SFL-LEAP is the convention Sailing Frog's Leap uses so my software can recognize and organize LEAP records consistently.
Why use a namespace? It lets LEAP software separate these records from unrelated wallet activity and organize them into badges, events, challenges, the Crew Record, and administrative history.
